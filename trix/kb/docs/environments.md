title: NASA HRP - Five hazards of human spaceflight (by mission environment)
url: https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/
---
Health monitoring changes with where the crew is. On a space station in low Earth orbit the crew is weightless, Earth's magnetic field blocks part of the radiation, flight surgeons answer in seconds and return to Earth takes hours.

On a base on the Moon, gravity is about one sixth of Earth's, so bones and muscles still carry less load than on Earth. There is no magnetic shield, so a solar storm means going to the shelter. Lunar dust can irritate the eyes, skin and lungs after surface walks.

In a rover away from the base, the crew has only a small medical kit and a small closed cabin where carbon dioxide can build up quickly. A health problem in a rover usually means driving back to base.

In a spacecraft in deep space on the way to Mars, the crew is weightless for months, radiation exposure is higher, messages to Earth can take about 20 minutes each way and there is no quick return, so the crew must assess and treat problems on board.
