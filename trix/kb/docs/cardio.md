In weightlessness fluid shifts toward the upper body. When astronauts return to gravity, blood pools in the legs and many feel faint on standing (orthostatic intolerance); female crewmembers are more susceptible.

Countermeasures include gradient compression garments and fluid loading before landing. A stand test (heart rate and blood pressure lying down, then standing) shows how much help someone will need after landing.

In flight, a rising heart rate at rest, changes in blood pressure, or an irregular rhythm should be tracked against the astronaut's own baseline. An irregular rhythm with dizziness or chest symptoms is treated as an emergency.
