id: bone
title: NASA HRP - Risk of Spaceflight-Induced Bone Changes
url: https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-spaceflight-induced-bone-changes/
---
In weightlessness, weight-bearing bones such as the spine and hips no longer carry the body, and on average lose about 1 to 1.5 percent of their density each month on four to six month missions.

This loss raises the risk of fractures and early osteoporosis. It is silent: crew cannot feel it, so trends and countermeasures matter.

Resistive exercise is the main countermeasure. Adequate calcium, vitamin D and energy intake support it, and NASA studies suggest bisphosphonate medicines may reduce some loss.

Bone density scans are done on Earth before and after flight because there is no bone scanner onboard; in flight, TRIX tracks exercise load and urine calcium as a proxy.
