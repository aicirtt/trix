id: sleep
title: Barger et al. 2014 - Sleep deficiency in astronauts (Lancet Neurology)
url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4188436
---
A ten-year study of more than 4,000 nights in space found astronauts averaged about six hours of sleep per night on the ISS, even though 8.5 hours were scheduled.

Sleep-promoting medicines were widely used. Sleep loss reduces alertness and performance, which matters for safety-critical tasks.

Countermeasures include protecting the sleep window, controlling light before sleep, keeping schedules regular, and moving demanding work away from times of high fatigue. Because short sleep is common in space, alerts should be based on each astronaut's own pattern and on runs of very short nights, not on one short night.
