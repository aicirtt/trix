title: NASA - Decompression sickness and spacewalk injury risks

NASA lists the risk of decompression sickness and the risk of injury and compromised performance from spacewalks among the human health risks of spaceflight.

Spacesuits run at a lower pressure than the cabin, so nitrogen dissolved in the body can form bubbles if it is not removed first; crews breathe pure oxygen before a spacewalk (pre-breathe) to reduce this risk.

After a spacewalk, joint pain, tingling, numbness, itching skin or unusual tiredness can be signs of decompression sickness and should be reported to the crew medical officer straight away.

Working in pressurised gloves can injure fingertips and fingernails, and suit work strains the shoulders, so hands and shoulders are checked after every spacewalk.
